Bayesian Product Discovery

Vanity metrics feel like control. They don’t make decisions easier.

This tool is for disciplined mind-changing: start with an outside-view belief, then update it when a real test comes back.

Each row below is one test. Say what happened, then answer two questions about that signal: how often you would see it if the bet were working, and how often you would see it anyway if the bet were failing. The gap between those two numbers is all the evidence you actually have.

In plain English, what are you claiming will happen?
Argue your own number first. Then check it against a published base rate — the gap is worth a conversation.

Weight of evidence is the ratio of those two columns. Above 1 the belief rises; below 1 it falls; at roughly 1 the signal told you nothing, however good it felt. That is the working definition of a vanity metric.

How the update works

Each test takes the belief you walked in with and re-weighs it by how much better the signal predicts success than failure. The result becomes the starting belief for the next test.

belief after = (belief × chance if it works) ÷ (belief × chance if it works + (1 − belief) × chance if it fails)

In standard notation: P(H|E) = P(H)·P(E|H) / [ P(H)·P(E|H) + (1−P(H))·P(E|¬H) ]. The first column of the table is the sensitivity P(E+|H); the second is the false positive rate P(E+|¬H), which is 1 − specificity.
